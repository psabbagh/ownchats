# OwnChats design, v0

Written 2026-11-04, before any code. Update it as decisions change.

## What it is

Your own Instagram comment-to-DM bot. Someone comments a keyword on one of your Reels. OwnChats replies under the comment and sends them a DM with your link, and can ask for their email first. It runs on your own Cloudflare account (free tier) with your own Meta app, so there is no per-contact pricing and the leads are yours.

It was built for the author's own account and is shared as-is. Take it, change it, run it. No support is promised.

## Who it is for

- Creators who will follow a guided setup of about an hour to stop paying per contact.
- Not for people who want a one-click "Connect Instagram" with no setup. A hosted tool is the right choice for them. A hosted OwnChats would mean one Meta app through App Review for everyone, billing and support, and is out of scope.

## Scenarios in v0

1. **Keyword, then DM.** A comment containing the keyword on a chosen Reel, or on any post, gets a private reply DM and a public reply under the comment ("Sent! Check your DMs, it may be under message requests."). The DM opens with a button ("Send me the link"). The tap counts as the person replying, which is what lets OwnChats send a second message. The button can be turned off per campaign; the next step then goes in the first DM. A new campaign starts from one of two flows, picked at the top of the form: the link in the first DM with no email ask, or the button, whose tap brings the link and asks for the email. Both are only starting points, and the follow check is off in both.
2. **The email, in one of three modes per campaign:**
   - **Link only.** The tap sends the link. No email ask.
   - **Link plus optional email.** The tap sends the link right away, on its own. When the person first opens it, a second message invites them to reply with their email ("Want more like this? Reply with your email and I'll send you my next ones as they come out. Unsubscribe anytime."). The ask goes out once, and only within 24 hours of the tap; someone who never opens the link, or opens it later, is not asked. Where no second message can follow (the link in the first DM with no button) or no open can be seen (click counting off), the ask goes under the link in the same message. When the last thing OwnChats sent the person, within the last day, is another campaign's ask they haven't answered, no second ask goes out: their email, when it comes, is saved for every campaign waiting on one, so each guide reaches their inbox. Someone who already gave their email on an earlier campaign is not asked again: the ask becomes an offer with one button ("Want this one in your inbox too? I'll send it to the email you gave me before." [Email it to me]). The tap saves that email for this campaign under the consent they gave then, the lead webhook sends the guide, and "Done. Check your inbox." follows, with no privacy button and nothing after. Without a lead webhook no email could follow, so nothing is offered. The three texts are one wording for every campaign, in Settings. A valid email reply is saved as a lead with its consent wording and gets a thank-you, with a button to the privacy page when the campaign has a label for it. An email sent before the ask is not saved, since nothing was asked yet. Anything else gets no re-ask and no gate, and silence is fine.
   - **Email first.** The tap brings the email question, which says what the person will receive. A valid email is saved and the link is sent. Anything else gets one re-ask; after a second miss the link is sent anyway, so nobody who commented leaves empty-handed. A message with no email in it counts as an answer only within 24 hours of the question. After that it is an ordinary DM and gets no reply, while an email is still taken for as long as the conversation stays open.
3. **Follow check.** Per campaign, off by default and not recommended, because Meta's Spam standard (https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/) lists requiring follows to gain access to specific content as like/share-gating; the form says so beside the option. After the tap, OwnChats asks Meta whether the person follows the account. Following: the email step or the link, as without the check. Not following: "Follow me first, then tap the button" with an "I'm following" button. A tap (or any reply) that still shows no follow gets a second message ("I can't see your follow yet"), and the next one gets the link either way, so nobody who commented leaves empty-handed and someone Meta is slow to see isn't stuck. If Meta's check fails or leaves the answer out, the link goes out: our error never blocks anyone. It needs the opening button, since Meta only answers once the person has tapped or written. A DM keyword is checked at once, since the person wrote first. If Meta refuses the opening button in a private reply, the plain-text fallback sends the link without a check.
4. **DM keyword.** Someone sends the keyword by DM (for example, their comment is older than 7 days) and gets the same flow, without the button, since their message already opened the conversation. Off by default and switched on per campaign, because the keyword matches anywhere in a DM: with TRAFFIC as the keyword, "how do I get more traffic?" would get the link.

Matching rules: case-insensitive, optional extra spellings per campaign (for example AGENTS and AGENT). A comment is answered when it is the keyword, give or take a few words: emoji, punctuation, @mentions and repeats of the keyword don't count, and up to 3 other words may sit around it. So "listen listen listen", "Listen 🙌" and "yes listen please" match LISTEN, while "So now I have to read instead listen?" does not; a longer comment like that goes to To check (below) for the owner. Spacing doesn't matter in such a comment: "list en", "l i s t e n", "set-up" with any kind of dash, and "setup" for SET UP all match. In a keyword of 5 letters or more, one letter may be off ("lisen", "listten", "litsen"); a shorter keyword must be exact, so GEAR never answers "year". A comment that spells a keyword exactly wins over one a letter off another campaign's keyword. A keyword with no letters or digits, such as 🙌, matches anywhere in a comment, as before. The DM keyword keeps the older rule: whole word, anywhere in the DM. A campaign on one chosen post can instead answer every comment ("any comment" mode); on all posts that would DM every commenter on the account, so it is not offered. A campaign watches one post or all posts, or waits for the next post (below); a campaign on one post wins over an all-posts campaign, and a keyword campaign wins over an any-comment one. Comments by the account owner are ignored, and so are comments written before the campaign was created, so arming a Reel never messages its old commenters. One DM per person per campaign, however many times they comment.

Shortcut keywords: one list for the whole account, set in Settings and empty by default, of what the audience comments to mean "send it", such as 🙌. Every keyword campaign on one post answers them on top of its own keywords. They are tried only when no campaign takes the comment by its own keyword or as any comment, and then only for a campaign on the post commented on, so "AGENTS 🙌" still goes to the AGENTS campaign and a 🙌 under a post with no campaign gets nothing. An emoji shortcut counts anywhere in a comment; a word shortcut (SKILL) follows the comment rule and must be spelled exactly, so "skill please" is answered and "what skill do I need for this?" or "I still don't get it" is not. Campaigns on all posts don't answer them. A shortcut the campaign only shares through Settings never attaches it to its post, but a word the campaign lists as its own keyword does, even when it is also a shortcut, so a Reel can still ask for SKILLS. A shortcut never counts as a DM keyword, even in a campaign that lists it itself. To check lists a skipped shortcut as matching now, while near misses are still looked for against a campaign's own keywords only.

The next post: a campaign can be set up before its Reel exists, since comments arrive in the first minutes after posting. It then waits, answering nothing. While a campaign waits, the Cron reads the account's latest posts through a 10-minute cache, and at once when a comment arrives on a post OwnChats has never seen (in neither that list nor the hourly post list), which is how a new post shows itself; it attaches the campaign to the first post published after the moment it was saved with that choice whose caption asks for its keyword: "comment", then the keyword, with only punctuation, emoji or "the word" between ("Comment AGENTS", "Comment the word AGENTS"). A caption that only mentions the word ("my agents bring traffic") doesn't count. So several campaigns can wait at once, each for its own Reel, and a keyword used on an older Reel doesn't get in the way: only posts published since the campaign started waiting count, and the old campaign stays on its old Reel. An any-comment campaign has no keyword to look for and takes the first new post. Saving a campaign again while it waits keeps the moment it started waiting. Comments that reached the post before it attached were skipped as matching no campaign. Those the campaign answers go back through the pipeline in the same Cron run, so posting first and attaching a minute later misses nobody. A campaign that is off still attaches, but answers nobody, and its early comments wait under To check.

Refused sends: a campaign's page also lists, under To check, the people still waiting on something Instagram refused for a passing reason (a rate limit, an outage): a first DM after their comment, or the message after their tap or reply. The Cron retries both on its own (below); each entry says when it tries next, or that it stopped, and has a Send button while Meta's window lasts (7 days from the comment, 24 hours from the tap). Every entry also has "Copy reply", which marks the person answered by hand and shows the campaign's link message with the person's own tracked link, to paste in Instagram, so a click from a hand reply counts like one from a DM; and "Already answered by hand", for someone the owner wrote to already. Either one sends nothing, stops the retries, takes the person off the list, logs it, and counts them under the campaign as answered by hand (`answeredByHand` in `GET /api/campaigns`, a number only). People refused before automatic retries began, and people whose message after a tap can no longer be sent, are listed with that button only, for 7 days. The campaigns page counts them in "Needs you".

Missed comments: a campaign's page lists, under To check, the skipped comments it could still answer. These are comments that came close to its keyword (two typos, a plural, a word that starts with it, or one letter off or split in a longer comment), name it in a comment too long to be answered by itself, match it now (they came in while it was off, or before a spelling was added), or were written before it started. Each one is less than 7 days old and comes from someone this campaign hasn't answered. Each has an Answer button, and near misses also get "Add the spelling and answer". Nothing is answered without the owner's click. Each also has "Copy reply" and "Already answered by hand", as for refused sends below, for a comment the owner answers in Instagram: nothing is sent, the comment leaves To check and Needs you, and the person counts as answered by hand, with a conversation so a later comment gets no automatic DM. And "Ignore", for a comment not worth answering: nothing is sent, the comment leaves To check and Needs you, and nobody is counted. The comment stays skipped, under the reason "ignored on To check", which no list or count looks for, and the person can still comment the keyword and get the DM. These three work with the campaign off.

Public replies: each campaign has several reply variants (three by default). One is picked at random each time, never the same one twice in a row, because identical replies on a busy post are a known cause of Instagram comment bans. The public reply is posted only after the DM went out, so "Sent!" is always true.

Likes: when the owner turns it on in Settings (off by default), every comment a campaign answers also gets a like from the account, after its DM went out and before the public reply. It needs `instagram_manage_engagement` in the login configuration. A refused like changes nothing else: the DM is out and the public reply still goes. It is logged, and left out of the campaign's failed count and of what needs the owner, except a refusal for a missing permission, which Settings shows with the fix until a like works or the owner reconnects. Meta refuses comments by private accounts, so those get no like.

Everything OwnChats sends is editable per campaign, and a new campaign ships with defaults that work without editing:
- the public reply variants
- the first DM and its button label
- the message with the link, and up to three links, each with its button label (without labels, the links go in the message instead)
- link plus optional email: the email ask, the thank-you once the email is saved (empty: no thank-you), and the label of the button under it that opens the privacy page (empty: no button)
- email first: the question, the reply when the answer is not an email (sent once), and the message that comes with the link once the email is saved
- the consent wording saved with each lead (empty: the question the person answered)
- an optional email tag, sent to the lead webhook (below) so the owner's email tool can pick the sequence
- an optional title for what the first link leads to, shown on its own line under the link message when the campaign has a single link (so two guides sent to one person never look like one sent twice), and sent to the lead webhook with the person's link, so one email template can deliver any campaign's guide by name
- an optional video id, in the owner's own numbering (for example SF-14), served by the API so the owner's other tools can link a video to its campaign; letters, digits, dots, dashes and underscores

Any text can use `{name}` (the person's Instagram name when Meta shares it, otherwise their username, otherwise "there") and `{username}`. Before the person taps or writes, OwnChats knows only the username on their comment; it asks Meta for the name once, when they first tap or write.

## Meta constraints, checked 2026-11-04

- A private reply sends **one** message per comment, within **7 days** of the comment. More messages are allowed only after the person replies, and only within **24 hours** of their reply.
- Private replies are limited to 750 per hour per account.
- Every Graph API call the app makes counts against the app's own rate limit (error code 4, "Application request limit reached"), reads as well as sends. Meta reports how much of it is used on every answer, in the `X-App-Usage` header (and per account in `X-Business-Use-Case-Usage`). On 2026-11-21 every send Meta refused was this code, while background reads were over 90% of the calls.
- People who don't follow the account get the DM in their message requests, so the public reply says so.
- The account owner's own comments never trigger anything, so testing needs a second account.
- Connection: Instagram API with **Unified Login** (Facebook Login for Business with a configuration, no Facebook Page needed). Meta apps created since about 2026-10-08 can no longer choose "API setup with Instagram login", so a setup guide written for that path breaks on a new app. Unified Login needs a free Meta business portfolio, calls `graph.facebook.com`, and reportedly gives a token that does not expire. The account must be a Creator or Business account.
- The opening button is a button template sent as a private reply. Meta documents only plain text private replies, but other open-source tools send button templates this way. The first real run must confirm it; if it fails, the campaign falls back to sending the link in the first DM.
- Access level: Standard Access is enough, and no App Review. Meta's platform overview says an app that serves only your own account needs Standard Access; its webhook setup page says `comments` webhooks need Advanced Access. In practice the second doesn't apply to an owner who has a role on the app: on a published app with Standard Access, comment webhooks arrive, and since webhooks were turned on (2026-11-06) over 99% of comments came by webhook and fewer than 1% by poll. Private replies reach commenters with no role on the app (proven 2026-11-05). So webhooks carry comments, and the comment poll is only a backup for a missed webhook. Advanced Access also would not raise Meta's app-level rate limit (error code 4, 200 calls an hour per daily active user), so the poll has to stay well under that budget on its own. Advanced Access matters only if someone without a role on the app connects their own account; `docs/meta-app-review.md` walks through it.
- Tokens: Unified Login gives a "system-user" token. Set to expire "Never" in the app's configuration, it needs no refresh, and Meta offers no simple refresh for it anyway. OwnChats checks it once a day with `debug_token` and warns on `/setup` and the dashboard a week before any expiry Meta reports, or as soon as it stops working.
- Follows: Meta's user profile has `is_user_follow_business`, whether the person follows the account. Like the name, it is readable only after the person has interacted in the DM. Meta's docs list a typed message, an ice breaker and the persistent menu as that interaction, and don't mention a tap on a postback button, which the follow check relies on. It does answer after such a tap: tested on 2026-11-07.
- Likes: since 2026-04-22 an app can like a comment as the account, `POST /<IG_USER_ID>/likes` with `comment_id`, with `instagram_manage_engagement`, on Facebook Login only (which Unified Login is). Not comments by private accounts, nor Stories. More than 50 calls in 5 seconds locks the account out of it for an hour. Meta doesn't say whether the permission needs App Review.
- Ids: the id on a comment and the id DMs come from may differ. A private reply returns the person's messaging id, and OwnChats keys the conversation on that, while still recognising the commenter's id, so the tap finds its conversation and a second comment is still "the same person".

## Architecture

- One Cloudflare Worker in TypeScript, a D1 database, and a Cron trigger every minute.
- Routes:
  - `/webhook`: Meta's verification handshake and incoming events.
  - `/setup`: the setup checklist (below).
  - `/dashboard`: campaigns, leads and the activity log. The campaigns page opens with what needs the owner: people still waiting on a refused send, per campaign ("2 people tapped and got nothing, 14 h left to answer"), a check that failed, a post no waiting campaign attached to, and comments to check (the one running out first on top). Failures are not a to-do: the status line under it says what Instagram refused in the last 24 hours, its most frequent error code and what became of those people ("all answered"), with a link to any other failures in the log, public replies left out since the DM had already gone out. Every campaign is in one list, on or off, newest post first (a campaign waiting for its post on top, one on all posts by its start date), each with its post's cover image from the hourly post list, an on/off switch, and a line for deciding whether to keep it on: how old its Reel is, its last matching comment and when, and its DMs in the last 7 days. Those that are off are dimmed and marked Off. Each row says when it started in plain words (today, yesterday, 3 days ago, Nov 2), with the exact time on hover. What a row that is on costs in Meta calls (its post's comment checks, which turning it off saves) is in the switch's tooltip and in the question before turning it off, and when a row that is off was turned off is in its Off tag's tooltip, so the row itself stays short. Rows quiet for a week carry a "No comment in N days" tag. Each row shows its numbers on one line and a chart of first DMs per day over 14 days. Filters above the list show All, On, Off or Quiet 7 days, with counts. Each card is a funnel of people, each counted once: asked, answered (by OwnChats, and by hand), tapped, got the link, clicked, gave their email, and "Waiting on you" only when someone is. A campaign opens on its results: Instagram's numbers for its post, one People section (who asked, who was answered and how, who waits; the funnel from comments and from the DM keyword; the post's comments in a line; why comments were skipped; and, folded away for diagnosis, Meta's refusals attempt by attempt), and every comment on the post with what happened to it. The form is behind Edit.
  - `/api`: token-protected JSON for integrations. Reads take the API token; creating a campaign for the next post and changing one's name, link title or keywords (`docs/campaign-api.md`) and sending a test lead to the lead webhook (`POST /api/test-lead`, `docs/lead-webhook.md`) take a separate write token.
  - `/privacy` and `/data-deletion`: the pages Meta requires, served by the app itself.
  - `/export/leads.csv`: a revocable secret link for Google Sheets.
  - `/go/<id>`: campaign links, counted (below).
- Comment pipeline: a comment arrives by webhook or poll, is deduplicated by comment id, is matched to a campaign, then gets the private reply and, once that went out, the public reply, and is recorded. Private replies are capped at 700 an hour (Meta's limit is 750); comments over the cap wait in a queue that the Cron drains, still inside the 7-day window. A private reply Meta refuses for a passing reason (a rate limit, an outage) is tried again 1, 5 and 30 minutes, then 1, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 48 hours after the first refusal, and a last time about 6.5 days after the comment, inside Meta's 7: 10 tries. Each wait gets a little random delay (a tenth of the wait, 1 to 20 minutes), so a burst of refusals doesn't come back in the same minute, and none lands within 15 minutes of the window's end. After the last, it waits under To check. The activity log says which try each refusal was and when the next one is, in the owner's time zone ("try 3 of 10, next at 9:40 am").
- Sends first: answers from Meta carry usage headers, the app's limit in one and each account's in the other, not always both. OwnChats keeps each limit's latest reading on its own and goes by the highest one less than 5 minutes old. Above 70% of a limit, the Cron skips the reads that can wait: insights, account days, the hourly post list, the account counts, and the comments of posts older than 2 days. Above 90%, it skips every background read. When Meta refuses any call for a rate limit (codes 4, 17, 32, 613, 80002, or HTTP 429), all background reads stop for 10 minutes, or for the time Meta gives, up to an hour, and so do retries of refused sends: one that falls due during the pause goes after it, before any read. A send by webhook (a comment, a tap) is never held back. The dashboard says when the limit is near or reached. A waiting comment keeps the campaign it was queued for. A comment the owner answers from To check goes back through the pipeline for the campaign they chose: its keyword and start date are not checked again, and every other rule still applies. Only comments skipped as "no matching campaign" or "written before the campaign started" can be handed over this way. OwnChats never sent those a private reply, so a click can't make it reply twice, and Meta refuses a second private reply anyway.
- Budget: the Workers free plan allows about 50 outgoing requests and 50 D1 queries per invocation. Each webhook delivery is its own invocation; the Cron run handles what it can and leaves the rest queued for the next minute. Comments that are skipped on sight are settled together in one batch.
- Post numbers: every hour the Cron lists the account's posts from the last 90 days (up to 150, three pages) into `media_stats`. Every 5 minutes it reads the follower, following and post counts into settings, one call, so a tool that shows the follower count sees it move. Each run then reads Instagram's lifetime insights (views, reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, reposts) for the two most overdue posts, and for a Reel its average watch time and skip rate, each in a request of its own. Reposts rides in the main request; if Meta refuses it, the request is sent again without it, so it never costs the other numbers. A post is due by its age, a campaign's post like any other: every 15 minutes in its first 2 days, hourly until day 8, every 6 hours until day 30, then daily; a campaign post Meta gave no posting time for, hourly. A Reel's watch time and skip rate come with those reads only every 6 hours in its first 2 days, then daily. Pages and the API only read the table. A failed read keeps the last numbers; a refused or failed watch time or skip rate keeps its last number and costs none of the others. If Meta refuses for lack of `instagram_manage_insights`, the pages say how to add it, and a reconnect makes every post due again. When a read works again after Meta blocked the whole app, every post and account day whose last read failed is due again at once, once per block. The daily cleanup drops posts older than 90 days unless a campaign still watches them.
- Account days: each Cron run reads one of the account's days from Meta into `account_days`: its reach split by follow type (followers, non-followers, unknown) and its follows and unfollows, in one request (`metric_type=total_value`, `breakdown=follow_type`). These are Meta's days, midnight to midnight Pacific time, whatever the owner's time zone, because Meta counts no other way. A day is read once it has ended, then every 6 hours until 3 days after its end, since Meta fills a day in over 48 hours (follows come late); after that it is left alone. The newest due day goes first, so a new install fills yesterday, then a day a minute back to 30 days ago, which gives another tool a month of follower history at once. A number Meta has not given yet stays null. A failed read keeps the day's last numbers. The daily cleanup drops days older than 90 days.
- Polling covers the posts that campaigns watch: each one-post campaign's Reel, and the 10 most recent posts when an all-posts campaign exists. Each is read by its age, most overdue first, up to 6 a run: every 15 minutes in its first 2 days (or when its age is unknown), every 30 minutes until day 8, hourly until day 30, then every 3 hours. Each post adds 0 to 4 minutes of its own to every wait, the same each time, so posts drift onto different minutes. While Meta's webhooks look unhealthy for comments, a post in its first 2 days is read every run instead, until they recover (see the decision of 2026-11-21 below). Webhooks bring comments as they are written; polling is the backstop for the ones Meta never delivers. A campaign waiting for the next post watches nothing until it is attached; while one waits, each Cron run starts by looking for its post (see The next post). Comments older than 7 days are ignored without a log entry.
- DM pipeline: a small state machine per person and campaign. States: opened (first DM with the button sent), asked to follow and asked again (follow check), awaiting email and re-asked once (email first), awaiting open (link sent, the optional email ask follows its first open), optional email (link sent and email asked for, an email reply is still welcome), done. Each conversation also keeps how many times the person was asked to follow and whether they followed at the last check, so a campaign can show who already followed, who was asked, and who followed after the ask. It sends only in answer to something the person just did (a tap or a message), or, for the optional email ask, when they open their link within 24 hours of that, so every follow-up is inside Meta's 24-hour window. A follow-up Meta refuses for a passing reason (or that finds no room left in the run) is kept on the conversation with what it was, and the Cron sends it on the same schedule as a private reply (1, 5 and 30 minutes, then 1, 3, 6 and 12 hours after the first refusal), with a last try about 21.5 hours after the person's tap or message, in the last tenth of Meta's 24 hours: 8 tries, never past the window; after that it waits under To check. A first DM by DM keyword is not kept: its claim is released, and the person can write again. A message written instead of tapping the opening or follow button counts as the tap when it comes within an hour of the button, or, at any time, when the whole message is the button's words or the campaign's keyword, ignoring case, punctuation and emoji; anything else is an ordinary DM to the owner, logged as a skipped tap. A message with no text (a shared post or Reel, a photo, a voice note, a sticker) never answers anything. While an optional email is pending, a message that is not an email is left alone, so a keyword for another campaign still works. Typed messages only reach conversations on campaigns that are on. A button tap works either way, since it answers a button OwnChats already sent.
- Every action is idempotent and logged with an outcome and a reason: sent, skipped (already sent, expired, own comment, no campaign), or failed (with Meta's error).
- Lead webhook: when a campaign saves an email, a `lead.created` event is queued in `deliveries` and sent right after the DM that answers the email, so the person never waits on it. It carries the campaign's first link as the DM sends it to that person (their own `/go/` link when clicks are counted, created before the link DM when the email comes first, so both carry the same one) and the link's optional title. It is a signed POST to the URL the owner sets in Settings: HMAC-SHA256 over `<timestamp>.<raw body>` with the owner's secret, in `X-OwnChats-Signature`, a fresh timestamp and signature on each attempt, and one delivery id kept across retries. A 2xx is done; a 429, a 5xx, a timeout or no connection is retried after 1, 5 and 15 minutes, then 1, 3, 6 and 12 hours (8 attempts over about 22 hours); any other reply is final. The Cron sends what is due, up to five a run, even while Instagram is disconnected. Every attempt is logged. Deleting a person cancels their waiting deliveries and queues a `lead.deleted` for each of their emails. Once settled, a delivery's payload is emptied. The format is in `docs/lead-webhook.md`.
- Owner alerts: an alert URL in Settings gets a short message when something needs the owner, and another when it's fixed. Checked by the Cron every 5 minutes, at the start of each run and even while Instagram is disconnected, from what OwnChats already records: no Meta call. The problems: Meta blocking every call from the app ("API access blocked", code 200 subcode 5258005, seen in the account read or the check for missed comments, opened at once), comments stopped arriving (the check for missed comments found at least 3 comments in the last 3 hours that no webhook brought, and more than the webhooks did), the check for missed comments failing or held back for 30 minutes while a campaign's post is under 2 days old, no tap or typed reply for 3 hours after at least 10 first DMs with a button, a webhook field (comments, messages, messaging_postbacks) missing from the account's subscription, read daily, Meta's webhooks refused for 15 minutes (signature or app secret), the token failing its daily check or Instagram disconnected, people waiting over 30 minutes on a send Meta refused for a passing reason, refused sends the Cron has stopped retrying, a post up 15 minutes with no campaign while one waits, and a burst of DM failures (5 people in an hour, for reasons that aren't retried). A problem opens after its grace period and is fixed once it has been gone for 30 minutes; while open it is reminded at most every 12 hours; at most 10 messages go out in any 24 hours, the tenth saying so, and a problem the cap kept quiet gets no "fixed". What is open is kept in one setting (`alert_state`). Messages go through `deliveries` with the lead webhook's retries, cut to 4 attempts over about 20 minutes. Slack and Discord webhook URLs get the JSON their chat expects; any other URL gets plain text with ntfy's Title, Click, Tags and Priority headers, or JSON when the owner picks it. The titles a person reads start with "OwnChats:", since the channel is often shared (healthchecks.io's ntfy integration posts to the same topic); the JSON keeps the bare title. Alerts name no one: an ntfy topic is readable by anyone who knows its name. "Send a test alert" sends one outside the cap. A heartbeat URL, optional, gets a GET at most every 10 minutes for a healthchecks.io-style service, sent only once a Cron run has answered the comments waiting and checked for missed ones, so the pings stop when OwnChats stops answering, and not only when the Worker stops running. A run that throws before that POSTs the error to the URL with `/fail` added, at most every 10 minutes, which marks a healthchecks.io check down at once. Both URLs are stored encrypted. The check runs every 5 minutes whether or not an alert URL is set; the URL only decides whether OwnChats sends the messages itself. `GET /api/health` (read token) serves the same problems to a watcher outside OwnChats: `ok`, each open alert with its title and the message saying what to do, and `checked_at`, when the Cron last checked, so a watcher also sees a Cron that stopped.
- Click counting: each campaign link in a DM goes out as `<base URL>/go/<id>`, with one random 16-character id per conversation and link (`tracked_links`), and the Worker answers with a 302 to the real link. The id is all the URL carries. A link sent again to the same person keeps its id. The first GET from a browser sets the link's first click and logs it; HEAD requests, link previews and crawlers (Meta's `facebookexternalhit` and `meta-*` agents, which draw Instagram's link previews, iMessage, Slack, Discord, search engines, scripts, and requests with no user agent) are redirected without counting. A campaign counts people who clicked at least one of its links, once each. Deleting a person deletes their ids; deleting a campaign detaches them, so links already sent keep working and count nowhere. Counting can be switched off in Settings; links then go out as they are.
- Dashboard access: an admin password, chosen on the Deploy to Cloudflare screen and kept as a Worker secret (`ADMIN_PASSWORD`). Choosing it at deploy time means nobody can claim a fresh deployment before its owner does. Sign-in with the connected Instagram account can come later. Optionally, Cloudflare Access replaces the password page (README, "Signing in"): with `ACCESS_TEAM` and `ACCESS_AUD` set, every page needs Access's signed token, checked by the Worker itself, and the password stays only to encrypt stored secrets.
- Secrets: the Meta app secret and the access token are entered in `/setup` after deploying (Meta needs the Worker's URL first, so they cannot be asked for on the deploy screen). They are stored in D1 encrypted with a key derived from `ADMIN_PASSWORD`, never in the repo. Changing the admin password means entering the Meta values again.

1. **Deploy to Cloudflare** button: copies the repo into their GitHub, creates the D1 database, and gives them a URL. Pushes to their copy redeploy automatically.
2. **Open `/setup`**: a checklist that checks itself. For each Meta step it shows the exact value to paste (webhook URL, verify token, redirect URL, privacy URL) and turns green once the step really works. The Instagram tester invite is explained where it matters: it lets a second account test before the app is published.
3. **Connect Instagram** from the checklist.
4. **Test**: comment the test keyword from another account and watch it arrive and the DM go out.
5. **Create the first campaign** in the dashboard.

The README also carries a prompt for people who prefer an AI agent to walk them through it, and `docs/guide-for-ai-agents.md` holds what the first real setup taught: what Meta's screens actually show, the problems and their fixes, diagnosing from the database, and updating a deployed copy. A Deploy-button copy shares no history with this repo, so updating applies the upstream diff since the copy was made; the guide's commands were tested on a real copy.

## Exports and integrations

- v0: CSV download; Google Sheets through `=IMPORTDATA("<secret link>")` (refreshes roughly hourly, revocable); the lead webhook, a signed POST of each new email (and each deletion) to a URL the owner sets, which an email tool, Zapier, Make or a small script of the owner's own can take from there; the JSON API with a token, which is how a personal knowledge base or any other tool reads campaigns, leads and counts. `/api/campaigns` includes each campaign's post numbers, where it listens (`attachment`: its post, the next post, or all posts, with `waiting_since`), whether a link, its title and an email ask are set, its people, each counted once (`asked`, `answeredAutomatically`, `answeredByHand`, `tapped`, `waitingOnYou`, and `refused` with `people`, `recovered`, `answeredByHand`, `waiting` and `unreached`), its failed attempts, for diagnosis, split into DMs that didn't go out (`failedDms`), public replies Instagram refused after the DM (`refusedPublicReplies`) and lead webhook deliveries given up (`failedLeadWebhooks`), and `warnings` for set-ups that fail silently, `/api/posts` serves every post from the last 90 days with its numbers and campaigns (`since=` filters by posting date), `/api/activity` each step with its reason and, in `detail`, Meta's own message and error code for a refusal, `/api/account` the follower count, and `/api/account/days` the account's reach and follows for each of Meta's days (`since=` a date). So a tool that tracks the same video on several platforms takes Instagram's side from there. OwnChats keeps only the latest numbers; history belongs to that tool.
- Later: newsletter tools by pasted API key (Kit, Beehiiv, MailerLite), and an MCP server so an AI assistant can run campaigns.

OwnChats knows nothing about any particular integration. Integrations call the API, or take the webhook, from their own side.

## Consent and data

- The email ask says what the person will receive and that they can unsubscribe. The defaults don't name the sender or link the mailing address, both of which Canada's CASL asks of the line people answer: the DM comes from the sender's own account, and the thank-you's button opens the privacy page, which shows the name and mailing address from Settings (decision of 2026-11-11). Owners adjust the wording for where they and their audience are.
- Each lead is stored with the consent wording shown and a timestamp, and can be deleted on request through the data-deletion route. The lead webhook carries both, and a deletion is sent on as `lead.deleted`.
- Deleting a person removes their lead, conversations and log entries, and the text, username and id on each of their comments. Each comment keeps only its own id until the 30-day cleanup, so the poll and redelivered webhooks never take it for a new comment, store the person again or try to answer it twice. A new comment from them afterwards is a new request and is answered.

## Decisions

- 2026-11-04: Unified Login only, since every new Meta app gets it. The Graph host stays configurable so the older Instagram Login path can be added later.
- 2026-11-04: from the must-have research (ranked list in the author's notes), v0 adds the opening button, rotating public replies, all-posts targeting and any-comment mode. Follow gate, story triggers, newsletter push and click tracking stay out.
- 2026-11-04: the email ask is a per-campaign choice of three modes: link only, link plus optional email, email first. This replaces the earlier two scenarios (link, or email before the link).
- 2026-11-04: every outgoing text, button label and link is editable per campaign, with defaults and `{name}` / `{username}` placeholders. Public replies are picked at random rather than in turn.
- 2026-11-05: a campaign opens on its results rather than its form, so checking a Reel needs no database query. Comment counts cover 30 days, since comment rows are deleted after that. The DM funnel counts conversations, which last as long as the campaign. Taps in the last 24 hours, links sent again and failures come from the log, which keeps 90 days.
- 2026-11-05: the DM keyword is off by default for new campaigns, the setup test campaign included. It matches anywhere in a DM, so a keyword that is a common word answers ordinary conversations.
- 2026-11-05: missed comments are answered from the dashboard, on the owner's click, rather than with a database update. Near misses are found by edit distance (swapped letters count as one typo), by joined or split words, and by words that start with the keyword. Short keywords allow no typos, so GEAR doesn't flag "year" or "hear".
- 2026-11-06: each campaign shows its post's views and reach, read by the Cron and stored, so a page view costs no Meta call and a share of views can be shown next to the DMs. OwnChats stays a comment-to-DM tool: a library of every post, trends over time and other platforms (TikTok, where there is no comment-to-DM) belong in the owner's own tools, which read these numbers from the API.
- 2026-11-06: OwnChats is the owner's Instagram source for their other tools: it lists every post from the last 90 days and the follower count, and serves them through the API. Posts are read by age, because a post's numbers move fast in its first week and hardly at all after a month, and the Meta calls stay within one or two a minute.
- 2026-11-07: a campaign can wait for the next post, so it is ready before the Reel goes up without the owner, or an agent, watching for the post. It attaches to the first post published after it was saved whose caption says "Comment" and its keyword, checked every minute, and answers the comments that came in first without a click: they are on the post it was set up for. The caption, not the order of posting, decides, so several upcoming Reels can be armed at once; the call to comment is used because the keyword must be exact there anyway, while a hook or title changes between script, video and caption. The form reads the post list fresh, so a Reel posted a minute earlier is listed rather than mistaken for the next one. It is stored as a waiting time on the campaign, so a waiting campaign is never taken for an all-posts one.
- 2026-11-06: deleting someone by username takes two clicks. The first shows what that username matches (emails, conversations, comments, log entries) and deletes nothing; the second deletes exactly the ids it showed. A typo in a typed username could otherwise delete another real person's data at once. The Delete button on a lead stays one click behind a confirm, since it names the person already.
- 2026-11-07: new emails leave OwnChats by a signed webhook rather than by an integration with any one email tool, so any tool, Zapier, Make or a small script of the owner's can take them, and OwnChats stays vendor-neutral. The payload carries what consent law asks the owner to prove (the exact wording and the time), the Instagram name only when Meta shared it (never the username, so the email tool's own greeting applies), an optional per-campaign email tag that picks the sequence, and the person's link with its optional title, so the email that delivers the guide needs no list of guides on the receiving side: each guide is configured once, in its campaign. One delivery id is kept across retries so the receiver can drop repeats; retries last about a day, since a lead that arrives a day late is still worth having and one that never arrives is lost. A deletion is sent on as `lead.deleted`, so the copy in the email tool goes too. "Send a test lead" sends a lead marked as a test, exactly as a real one, so the email side can be checked end to end without a comment. The default email lines now name the sender, link to a page with their mailing address and say people can unsubscribe, because the earlier default ("Want more like this? Reply with your email") met none of what Canada's CASL asks.
- 2026-11-07: campaign links go through the Worker as `/go/<id>` so a campaign can count who clicked, the one step of the funnel OwnChats couldn't see: a link tap never reaches it from Instagram. One random id per conversation and link, so the URL names nobody and a forwarded link can't be traced to anyone by its address. Only each person's first click counts, and previews don't, so the number reads as people, not fetches. Follow-ups after the link (a check-in DM, answers to count, a "needs a reply" list) were considered alongside and left out on the author's decision.
- 2026-11-07: pages show times in the owner's time zone, chosen in Settings, with its abbreviation ("2026-11-06 09:03 EST"). Until one is saved they show UTC and the campaigns page says so; the Settings list starts on the browser's zone. The API, the CSV and the database keep UTC, so other tools never have to guess a zone. A setting rather than the browser's zone, so the pages read the same from any device, and for an agent reading them.
- 2026-11-08: the campaigns page says what needs the owner, and a campaign can be switched on or off from it. Failures count for 24 hours, because the log can't be cleared and an all-time count would never go away. A post that goes up while a campaign waits and that no campaign attaches to is flagged, usually a caption without the "Comment KEYWORD" line: nothing else would show it, since the campaign just keeps waiting and the post's comments match nothing. The switch sends the state wanted rather than a flip, so a double click or an old tab can't undo it, and it asks before turning off only: a stray tap could otherwise silence a live campaign, while turning one on only affects comments still to come.
- 2026-11-07: a campaign can ask people to follow before it sends the link, because a Reel's call to action often says "Follow me and comment", and the follow is worth more to the owner than the extra tap costs. It reads Meta's `is_user_follow_business` after the opening button's tap, the first moment Meta allows it. Someone who doesn't follow is asked, then asked once more, then gets the link anyway: like the email-first mode, nobody who commented leaves empty-handed, and someone who did follow but whom Meta is slow to see isn't stuck. Keeping on asking until they follow was considered and left out. A failed check sends the link, since an error of ours must never block a person. The campaign page counts who already followed, who was asked and who followed after the ask, which is the number of followers the check gained.
- 2026-11-08: a message answers the email question only if it holds an email or comes within 24 hours of the question, and a campaign that is off no longer answers typed messages. Before this, a conversation left waiting for an email stayed open for a week, so a link the person shared with the owner days later got "that doesn't look like an email", even from a campaign that had been switched off. The week still applies to an email, which is plainly an answer, and to a reply in place of the opening or follow button, since that only leads to what the person asked for.
- 2026-11-08: a new campaign starts from one of two flows instead of a button with no email ask: the link in the first DM, or the button, whose tap brings the link and asks for the email. The first is preselected because it needs no consent wording. In the second the email stays optional, so nobody has to give one to get the link; asking for the email first remains a choice in the form. The follow check stays as a per-campaign option, off in both flows and not recommended: Meta's Spam standard names follows among the engagement that can't be required to gain access to specific content, and the check asks for one before the link, even though the link goes out in the end.
- 2026-11-09: a public reply Instagram refuses is reported as what it is and stays out of what needs the owner. It is only tried once the DM went out, so the person already has what they asked for and the owner has nothing to do. It stays in the activity log and in the campaign's failed count, which is where a run of refusals would show if Instagram started limiting the account. Subcode 33 points at the connection only on account-level calls: on a reply, what Meta can't reach is the comment, so it no longer tells the owner to reconnect. Code 20 is described as possible spam filtering, not as fact: Meta doesn't document the subcodes seen.
- 2026-11-10: a campaign has an optional video id of its own, served as `video_id` in `GET /api/campaigns`. The owner's video tracker had to read the id out of the free-text name, which an edit to the name breaks silently. It is free text in a narrow alphabet rather than a fixed pattern such as `SF-14`, since every owner numbers videos differently, and it is not unique: two campaigns for one video (a repost) are allowed.
- 2026-11-10: `GET /api/campaigns` says what a tool needs to check a campaign is ready before its video goes up, and names the set-ups that fail silently as `warnings` with a stable `code`: an email tag on a campaign that never asks for an email, an email ask with no tag, no link title or no lead webhook, and a campaign that is off. On 2026-11-10 every campaign of the author's showed 0 emails because none asked for one and the lead webhook was not set, and nothing said so. The checks live here because only OwnChats knows whether the webhook is set; what tag the owner's email tool listens for stays the tool's business. Failures are split by kind, because a refused public reply comes after the DM and needs nothing, and counting it with failed DMs overstated problems; `failed` keeps its old meaning so existing readers see no change.
- 2026-11-10: a campaign that waits for the next post can be created through the API, from the caption the post will carry, so the session that approves a video's captions also sets up its campaign. The keyword is read from that caption with the same rule that later attaches the campaign (or, when given, must be one the caption asks for), because a hand-typed keyword that differs from the caption's "Comment KEYWORD" was the one step per video that failed silently. A check endpoint answers whether a caption would attach given keywords, which campaigns already waiting it would also attach, and whether a post with that caption is already up, and saves nothing; `dry_run` does the same for a whole create. Creating is refused when a campaign with the same video id already waits, or when the caption would also attach one that waits, since the older campaign would win and the new one answer nobody. Only creating is offered: editing, switching off and deleting stay in the dashboard, where the owner sees what they change, until a tool needs them.
  Authorisation: a second token, the write token, made and revoked in Settings like the first and stored the same way, as a SHA-256 hash. It creates campaigns, checks captions and (since 2026-11-12) sends test leads, and reads nothing; the read token can't write. One token for both was considered and left out: the read token already lives in tools that only read (the owner's video tracker reads every hour), and a leak there must never be able to change what DMs go out to strangers. The write token, in turn, is handed to an agent that writes captions, which has no reason to read leads. Scopes on a single token would do the same with more to get wrong; two fixed tokens are what the owner's other tools already use. The check takes either token, since it shows nothing the read token can't. Every create is logged.
- 2026-11-11: in "link plus optional email", the email ask is its own message, sent when the person first opens the link, instead of a line between "Here you go:" and the link button. In one bubble the ask sat above the button, so the thing the person asked for was the last thing they saw, and everyone was asked whether or not they wanted the link. Asking at the first open reaches only people who opened it, while they still have the conversation in front of them. The first open is already recorded for click counting, so it costs one more message and no new tracking. The ask is not sent later by the Cron to people who never opened: that would be a nudge nobody asked for. The default ask is shorter and no longer names the sender or links to the mailing address, which reverses part of the 2026-11-07 decision: the DM comes from the sender's own account, and a long legal line with a raw URL made the ask read as a form. It still says what people will get and that they can unsubscribe, and the thank-you now carries a button to the privacy page, whose label is per campaign (empty: no button). The cost is that the address is one step further away than CASL's wording asks for at the moment of the ask; the form's hint says so, and owners can add to the wording.
- 2026-11-12: an owner can set their own defaults for new campaigns in Settings: the link button's label, the optional email ask, its thank-you and the thank-you's button. An owner who sends the same kind of thing every time (a guide, with an ask that offers to email it) had to rewrite those four on every campaign, and a campaign created through the API got OwnChats' wording until someone edited it. The built-in defaults stay neutral because they can't know what the link is or whether an email will follow: an ask that promises to email the link is false for an owner with no lead webhook. Copying the wording from the owner's latest campaign was considered and left out, since a one-off or test campaign would silently become the next default. An empty field means OwnChats' own wording, so "no thank-you" or "no button" can't be a default; those stay a per-campaign edit.
- 2026-11-12: shortcut keywords, one account-wide list in Settings that every keyword campaign on one post answers on top of its own keywords. The author's audience comments 🙌 to mean "send it", and on 2026-11-09 two such comments under a Reel got nothing because its campaign didn't list 🙌. Campaigns are armed by several agents and by hand, through the form, the API and the database, and a note in one agent's memory doesn't reach the others, so a setting inside OwnChats is the only default every path shares. It is empty by default, so other owners opt in. A shortcut is shared by every campaign, so it can't say which link someone wants; what it means is "send me what this post offers". So it counts only for a keyword campaign on the post commented on, and only when no campaign takes the comment by its own keyword: a comment that names a keyword still goes to that keyword's campaign. Campaigns on all posts don't answer shortcuts, since one would then send its link for a 🙌 under every post the account has; the form already asks for an all-posts keyword used for nothing else, which a shared shortcut can't be. For the same reason a shortcut never attaches a waiting campaign to its post and never counts as a DM keyword, and the setting overrides a campaign that lists a shortcut itself, so campaigns armed before the setting, which list 🙌 one by one, behave the same as new ones. Like any keyword, a shortcut matches anywhere in a comment, so "Love this 🙌" also gets the link; the author accepted that. `GET /api/campaigns` serves the list as `shortcut_keywords`, so an agent arming a campaign can check it rather than add 🙌 to each one.
- 2026-11-12: the message with the link joins the owner's defaults in Settings. It was left out of the first set on the reasoning that it describes one specific link, so it has to be written per campaign. In practice that meant campaigns armed by hand got a paragraph about their guide and campaigns armed through the API got "Here you go:", and the same account answered two Reels in two voices. One sentence that fits every link of the owner's ("Here's the step-by-step guide from my video:") is enough, since the button and the page say the rest, and a new campaign then needs only its keyword and its link.
- 2026-11-12: `POST /api/test-lead` does what Settings, "Send a test lead" does, so a script or agent can check the lead webhook chain, through the owner's email tool to the inbox, without driving the dashboard in a browser. It was the one step of setting up and checking an OwnChats copy with no API. It runs the form's own checks and code (`checkTestLead`, then `sendTestLead`), so the two can't send different payloads, and answers with the delivery id, the HTTP status and the start of the receiver's reply, which the dashboard shows only as a notice. It takes the write token, not the read token: it makes the owner's email tool send an email to any address the caller names, which is an action, and a tool that only reads must never be able to do that. A test lead is still never saved in Leads and counts against no campaign.
- 2026-11-13: a Reel's average watch time and skip rate (the share of views skipped within the first 3 seconds) are read and served by `GET /api/posts` and `GET /api/campaigns`, as `avg_watch_time_ms` and `skip_rate_percent`. Instagram names watch time as its most important ranking signal, and the skip rate is the one number that says whether a Reel's opening works, so an owner who posts often needs both next to views and reach. Meta gives them only for Reels and refuses a whole insights request over one metric it won't give, so each is asked in a request of its own, after the post's other numbers and only for posts listed as REELS or VIDEO: a refusal costs nothing else, and a Reel read costs three Meta calls instead of one, each counted in the run's budget. `follows` and `profile_visits` were tried the same day and Meta refuses both for Reels on v26.0, so they are not read.
- 2026-11-13: reposts for every post, and the account's daily reach split by follow type with its daily follows and unfollows, are read and served (`reposts` in `GET /api/posts` and each campaign's `post`; `GET /api/account/days`). The owner's analytics tool shows how a Reel travels beyond followers and how many accounts each day brings, and follows per day can't be had per Reel: Meta refuses `follows` for Reels, so a day's follows next to that day's reach is the closest number Meta gives. Reposts goes in the post's main request, not one of its own as the Reel numbers do, because every post type has it and asking separately would cost a call per read; a refusal of it falls back to the request without it. The account's days cost one Meta call a minute at most and none once the last 30 days have settled. Meta has no field for a Reel's length (`duration` is not a field on the media node in v26.0), so none is served.
- 2026-11-17: a comment is answered only when it is the keyword, give or take a few words, rather than whenever the keyword appears as a whole word. A skeptical comment, "What is the advantage? So now I have to read instead listen?", got the LISTEN link and a public "Sent! Check your DMs" under it. People who want the link comment the keyword alone, repeated, or with an emoji or a word or two, so up to 3 other words are allowed, and a longer comment that names the keyword waits under To check rather than being dropped, at the cost of one click. In such a short comment, spacing and one wrong letter in a keyword of 5 letters or more are answered automatically ("list en" and "lisen" were both left for a click before): the comment being short is what makes that safe, since the same letter off inside a sentence is how ordinary words get caught. Real words one letter from the keyword ("listed" for LISTEN) are accepted as a rare cost. Shortcut keywords keep matching anywhere, since the author chose that on 2026-11-12, and so does the DM keyword, which is off by default and already warns about it.
- 2026-11-17: word shortcuts follow the comment rule, and a campaign's own keyword attaches it even when it is also a shortcut. The author added SKILL and SKILLS to the shortcuts next to 🙌, since the audience comments them to mean "send it" under any Reel. Two parts of the 2026-11-12 design then got in the way. Shortcuts matched anywhere in a comment, which the author accepted for an emoji but which, for an ordinary word, would send the link and a public "Sent!" to "this skill is overhyped", the very failure the comment rule was made for that morning. So a word shortcut now needs a short comment that spells it exactly: no letter off, since "still" is one letter from SKILL and a shortcut is tried on every post; a longer comment that names it waits under To check. Emoji shortcuts still count anywhere. And shortcuts were removed from a campaign's keywords before attaching, so a Reel asking "Comment SKILLS" could not be armed at all (the API refused it, and the Cron would never attach it). The removal was there so a word every campaign shares could not attach one of them; a word one campaign lists itself has no such ambiguity, and a second waiting campaign asking for it is refused like any repeated keyword. So attaching now reads every keyword a campaign lists, and only shortcuts it merely shares are left out. Matching comments and DM keywords still leave shortcuts out of a campaign's own list, so campaigns that listed 🙌 before the setting behave as before.
- 2026-11-14: a flagged post has a "Not for a campaign" button that stops flagging it. The flag is shown for every untaken post while any campaign waits, so a post that never asked for a comment word (a question, a post with no guide) stayed flagged until the waiting campaign's own Reel went up, sometimes days, and its suggestion was to add another campaign's keyword to a caption it didn't belong in. Telling the two apart from the caption alone isn't possible, since a forgotten "Comment KEYWORD" line looks the same as no ask at all, so the owner says which. Only the flag is affected: the posts' ids are kept in one setting (the last 50), and a waiting campaign still attaches to the post if its caption later asks for the keyword.
- 2026-11-15: a post that went up with no campaign while one waits is an entry in the Campaigns list, next to the campaigns, instead of a paragraph in "Needs you". The entry shows the start of its caption, when it was posted with a link to it, and its two choices side by side: "Not for a campaign" (as before) and "Attach", which offers the campaigns that were already waiting when it went up. The banner's paragraph described one path (add the comment word to the caption, or pick the post in the campaign) while its button did another, which read as confusing; "Needs you" now keeps one line that points to the entries. Attach goes through the Cron's own attach, not the campaign form: the end state is the same as picking the post there, and comments already on the post that the campaign answers get their DM too. It accepts only a post that is still listed and a campaign it was listed for. Posts are listed only while a campaign waits, the same rule as before: with nothing waiting there is nothing to attach, and every post outside a campaign would fill the list. A post marked not for a campaign leaves the list but stays in the campaign form's post picker.
- 2026-11-21: sends come first, because Meta's app-level rate limit counts every call. The author's own watcher now reads `/api/health` and pushes through a server of its own, so OwnChats' alert URL was turned off on his copy to avoid every problem arriving twice, and through ntfy.sh, which refused it that morning. Before, no URL meant nothing was remembered, and `/api/health` had to check on the spot: a dozen queries on each call, every 2 minutes, and no grace period, so a scanner's stray POST to `/webhook` or a minute of failed polling would have reached the owner. Now the check costs every copy its dozen queries every 5 minutes, about 78,000 rows a day at the author's volume, the same as a copy with a URL already paid. A problem that opened with no URL set was never told, so setting a URL later sends no "fixed" for it, only a 12-hour reminder while it lasts.
- 2026-11-27: when Meta stops blocking the whole app, every post and account day whose last read failed is read again at once, instead of at its next read by age. A post keeps its failure note until a read works, and a post past 30 days is read once a day, so the morning after that day's block, 19 older posts still showed a failure although nothing was wrong. The first refusal for the block is remembered in a setting (`app_blocked_at`); the first read that works afterwards, often the account read 5 minutes later, makes every post and day with a stored problem due and clears the setting, so it happens once per block and costs no extra call to learn the block is over. Only those with a problem: a post read fine before the block has nothing to clear, and making every post due would hold back the 15-minute reads of new posts for most of an hour. The Cron reads them at its usual pace, two posts a run, inside the same rate-limit checks as any read, so a library of 100 posts clears in under an hour. Migration 0018 makes the same catch-up once for the notes stored as "missing permission" before the block had its own reason, since OwnChats can't tell those from a real one; a real missing permission is simply stored again.
